\chapter*{ABSTRACT}\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{ABSTRACT}
\begingroup\setstretch{1}\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
Students of the Faculty of Science and Mathematics (FSM), Diponegoro University, need information on academic and student service procedures, such as academic leave, diploma legalisation, and late tuition payment. This information is written in Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) documents, yet students still ask the staff directly, so the same questions are asked repeatedly and can only be answered during service hours. A chatbot based on Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can answer these questions, but conventional RAG treats SOPs as plain text, so its answers risk mixing steps from other SOPs, reversing the order of steps, or naming the wrong official. Therefore, this study develops an academic and student service chatbot in the E-Office system using Process-Aware Workflow GraphRAG. The method models each SOP as a directed workflow graph that stores the order of steps, the actors, the documents, and the outputs, then selects the relevant SOP before a locally hosted Large Language Model (LLM) composes the answer. The system is developed with the waterfall model, and the chatbot is integrated into E-Office through a REST service. The evaluation uses 40 verified questions with key-fact completeness, step coverage, step-order errors, actor errors, refusal accuracy, and token count as metrics, together with black-box testing of the system features. The results show that the chatbot reaches a key-fact completeness of 0.955 compared with 0.712 for conventional RAG, covers every step without order errors, and uses 44\% fewer tokens than supplying all SOP documents. These results indicate that SOP selection and an explicit workflow representation enable a local LLM to give complete and correctly ordered procedural answers.

\noindent Keywords : Academic Chatbot, Retrieval-Augmented Generation, GraphRAG, Workflow Graph, E-Office
